01 — Who I am

Raised across three languages, working toward one goal.

I'm a senior at the University of Maryland finishing a B.S. in Information Science with a minor in Technology Entrepreneurship and Corporate Innovation. I grew up speaking English, Amharic, and Tigrigna, and I'm currently picking up Arabic — which probably explains why I'm drawn to work that's really about translation: turning messy data into something a business can act on, or turning a fundraiser into something a community actually feels.

I didn't come into data science through a straight line. Some of it came from coursework — database design, data manipulation, user-centered design. Some of it came from working directly with people: as a Registered Behavior Technician, where every session lives or dies on accurate data collection, and as a peer tutor, where I have to translate a concept until it actually lands for someone else. Leading two student organizations taught me the rest — that the numbers only matter if you can get people to act on them, and that means communicating clearly with a team, not just writing clean code.

02 — How I work

Problem-solving, in practice — not just on a slide.

On a data project

Purchase Intent Classification

The hard part of this project was never the algorithm — Random Forest is well-documented. The hard part was the data: real browsing sessions are messy, inconsistent, and nothing like a cleaned-up textbook dataset. Getting to a 93% ROC-AUC meant slowing down, testing features methodically, and being willing to throw out an approach that looked good on paper but didn't hold up.

As a club president

Running People for Tigray (P4T)

Planning three events a semester on a student-org budget means something is always at risk of falling through — a venue, a vendor, a timeline. As President, my job is to catch that early: lining up logistics for an art show with a local Tigrayan artist, coordinating a joint showcase with ESSA, and making sure fundraising still lands around $3,000 a semester no matter what changed that week.

As a peer tutor

Guided Study Sessions

Explaining a concept to a classmate who's stuck is a different skill than solving the problem yourself — you have to find the actual gap in understanding before you can fix it, the same instinct I use debugging a model that isn't performing the way it should. It's made me a more patient, more precise communicator on both sides of that equation.

03 — Community

Communication is the part of "data" that doesn't show up in a notebook.

As a volunteer

MCPS ESOL Program

I volunteer with Montgomery County Public Schools' English for Speakers of Other Languages program, helping multilingual students and families navigate school resources. Growing up speaking Amharic and Tigrigna at home made this personal before it was ever a resume line — I know what it's like to need someone to translate a system, not just a sentence.

Also involved with

National Society of Black Engineers (NSBE) · Code: Black · Association for Women in Computing

Member of three organizations that support Black and women students in tech — spaces I show up to both for the community and for the reminder that the technical skills mean more when the room around you is invested in your growth too.
