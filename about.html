Who I am

The oldest of five, and the family organizer.

I moved to Maryland from Ethiopia with my family, and I'm the oldest of five girls. When your family is starting over in a new country, the oldest sibling ends up keeping things on track. For me, that meant planning ahead, staying on top of what needed to get done, and looking out for my younger sisters. Being organized wasn't something I learned in a class. It's who I had to be.

That's a big part of why I love data. Most of the work is taking something messy and making it make sense for someone else, and I've been doing some version of that my whole life.

My path into data wasn't a straight line, and I think that's one of my strengths. As a behavior technician, I learned that good decisions depend on careful, honest data. As a Starbucks shift leader, I learned how to keep a team calm and moving during a rush. As a peer tutor, I learned how to explain something until it clicks. And leading People for Tigray taught me that a plan only works if people believe in it enough to show up.

I speak English, Amharic, and Tigrigna, and I volunteer helping multilingual students and families in Montgomery County schools, because I remember what it's like to be new to a system.

How I work

How I work, in real situations.

Solving problems

When the AI got it wrong

In my review analysis project, the AI's summaries sounded confident, and some of them were wrong. It said jackets run small when customers were actually saying the opposite. I built a simple check in code that counted the real complaints, caught the mistake, and redesigned the tool so code handles the numbers and AI only writes the descriptions. I'd rather find a problem myself than have someone else find it later.

Leading a team

Running People for Tigray

Planning about three events a semester on a student budget means something is always at risk: a venue, a vendor, a timeline. My job as president is to catch those problems early and keep everyone moving. Whether it's an art show with a Tigrayan artist or a joint showcase with EESA, we still land around $3,000 a semester for two schools in Tigray, and that only happens because the whole team shows up.

Communicating clearly

Tutoring, and talking with families

Explaining a concept to a classmate who's stuck is a different skill than solving the problem yourself. You have to find exactly where the confusion is before you can fix it. I use the same skill as a behavior technician when I share progress with families, and it's the same instinct I use when a model isn't doing what I expected: find the real gap, then fix that.

Working hard

Showing up, every week

Right now I'm balancing school, a behavior technician job, shifts at Starbucks, leading People for Tigray, and a Power BI certification. It keeps me organized and honest about my time. When I say I'll get something done, I get it done.
